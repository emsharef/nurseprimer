# The arc: a prologue, twelve nights and a morning

Adopted 2026-10-25 from a story-critic review of the plan's 17-chapter ladder (which front-loaded
computability, reached "how does ChatGPT work" at chapter 9, repeated the "proxy gets gamed" beat four times and
buried the thesis). This replaces plan section 5. Each night: a scene, a toy the reader operates, a moment they
break Wick themselves, the idea earned, a hook into the next night. Everything is scored on the Ward Board.

**The clock.** The Lyceum Company (fictional) lends the ward a brass speaking tube connected to an enormous
hosted model, on a twelve-night trial; at the end, the hospital board decides whether it replaces the night
line. The Ward Board becomes your evidence. You build Wick to understand what's inside the tube.

**Wick's parts** (each a method; each fails somewhere): tape, rulebook, knobs, wires, a map, a voice, a
clipboard, a role, hands, a gauge, and finally a handover bell: knowing where its knowledge ends.

**Story spine.** You start wanting a machine that knows; you end as the person who can say exactly where it
doesn't. Bea's scepticism moves from the machine to the claims about it; once, around night 8, she tells of a
call she got wrong. At Morning she lets you make the recommendation and signs beside you.

**The 2 a.m. message** is a test card on the Ward Board every night, not a refrain. It arrives for real only on
the twelfth night.

**Set pieces:** the Rogues' Gallery (every message that fooled Wick is kept and re-run against each new Wick);
the Night Shift wager; Wick learning to speak; the poisoned chart note.
